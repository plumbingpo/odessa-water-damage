Floods start in many ways: heavy rain, storm surge, a river over its banks, a dead sump pump, a sewer backup or a major plumbing failure, and all of them call for fast professional help. In nearly every case the water falls into Category 2, known as gray water, or Category 3, known as black water, which means contaminants, bacteria and other hazards that are a real health risk. For flood emergencies anywhere in Odessa, Odessa Restoration is available 24/7 to extract the water, disinfect affected areas and bring the property back safely.

The first job is getting the water out quickly, and our submersible and truck-mounted pumps are able to clear thousands of gallons every hour. Next our technicians, who hold IICRC certification, judge the level of contamination and choose a sanitizing plan that covers HEPA air filtration, antimicrobial application and disinfecting each surface that got wet. Anything unsalvageable comes out and is hauled off correctly, including wet insulation, drywall that took in contaminated water and waterlogged carpet padding.

Then the drying begins: high-velocity air movers and commercial dehumidifiers of the LGR (low-grain refrigerant) type go in to pull moisture out of the building's structural parts. Daily readings from calibrated moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera tell us when everything is fully dry, which is what keeps mold from growing. Every stage of the job is recorded for your claim: photographs, detailed moisture maps, logs of the equipment used and the scope-of-work reports that insurance adjusters require.

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors
  • Add downspout extensions so roof water empties well away from the foundation
  • Clear leaves and debris from storm drains and gutters, which lets water drain the way it should
  • Make sure the ground slopes away from your foundation on every side
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor
  • Being outside a designated flood zone does not rule out flooding, so think about flood coverage

Why Hire a Professional for Flood Cleanup?

There is much more to a flood cleanup than removing the water you can see. Floodwater carries bacteria, sewage, chemicals and other contaminants that are serious health hazards. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Beyond the health concerns, a poor flood cleanup often leaves hidden moisture that turns into severe mold growth within days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. Drying by measurement is the one dependable method for avoiding secondary damage, which can add thousands to the repair bill.

Warning Signs for Flood Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Seepage at door thresholds, window wells or cracks in the foundation
  • Heavy rainfall, a dead sump pump and water spreading across the basement
  • Flooring that squelches when you walk on it and walls damp to the touch
  • Roof or wall openings left by a storm, with rain pouring through
  • Dirty water rising out of floor drains or sewer lines into rooms you use
  • Stains along baseboards and lower walls that mark the flood level
  • Soaked landscaping that leaves water pooling against your foundation
