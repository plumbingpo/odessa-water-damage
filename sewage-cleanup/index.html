Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Signs You Need Sewage Cleanup in Odessa

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
